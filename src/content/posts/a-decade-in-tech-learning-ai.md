After a decade of working in tech, I’ve found something I want to spend a lot more time understanding: AI.

I’m interested in how it works, what makes it useful, and how to build with it thoughtfully. That curiosity keeps pulling me toward the fundamentals. I want to be able to follow an idea, question it, and understand the decisions behind it.

So I’m making a deliberate effort to move my learning, and eventually more of my work, in that direction.

## Starting with the gaps

I don’t have a degree, and my maths foundations need work. Going deeper into AI means being honest about both of those things.

A decade of building software gives me experience I can draw on. It also leaves plenty of room for subjects I haven’t studied properly. I expect to encounter ideas that take a few attempts to understand, and moments when I need to go back and learn something more basic first.

I want to work through that. When a gap shows up, I’ll try to understand what’s missing and spend time on it. Some days, that might mean building a small experiment. Other days, it might mean working through a piece of maths slowly until it starts to make sense.

AI interests me enough to make that effort feel worthwhile.

## Why now

AI keeps coming up in conversations about the tools we use, the products we build, and the way we work. There’s a lot to take in, and I see this as a good opportunity to understand it better.

I’m still figuring out exactly where this will lead. For now, I have a direction I’m curious about and a reason to give it sustained attention.

## Getting ready for #100DaysOfLearningAI

I’m starting **#100DaysOfLearningAI** soon.

Before Day 1, I’m gathering some context and putting together a broad agenda for the hundred days. I want a sensible starting point and an order that helps the ideas build on each other. I’ll share that plan once I’ve worked it out.

Then, each day, I’ll write about what I learned and try to explain it in my own words, as clearly as I can. A post might include:

- The question I was trying to answer.
- An idea or experiment I spent time with.
- What I understood well enough to explain.
- What’s still unclear and needs another look.

I want the notes to reflect my actual understanding, including the parts that are still taking shape.

## A reason to show up

Writing publicly is part of the commitment.

Having a post to write gives each day a little structure. It asks me to pause, make sense of what I’ve spent time on, and put it into words. If I struggle to explain something, that gives me a useful place to return to.

It also makes the promise feel more concrete. I want to build the habit of showing up every day, and a public record gives me a sense of responsibility to keep going. Even a small, honest learning note is a reason to sit down and do the work.

The [series page](/series/100-days-of-learning-ai/) will bring those notes together in day order. For now, the next step is finishing the agenda. Then it’s one day, one lesson, and one post at a time.
